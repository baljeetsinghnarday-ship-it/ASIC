# Digital Logic & RTL Design — Project Portfolio

## Overview

This repository documents my early hands-on work in **digital electronics and RTL design**, beginning with fundamental logic gates and progressing toward the design and simulation of an **8-bit Ripple-Carry Adder (RCA)** using Verilog.

The projects were developed as part of my self-directed exploration of electronics. My primary interest is in understanding how digital systems are built from the hardware level upward, particularly in areas such as **digital design, VLSI, ASIC design, and computer hardware architecture**.

Rather than starting with a large project, I built the designs incrementally so that each stage became a building block for the next.

# 2. Full Adder

After working with basic logic, I implemented a **Full Adder** in Verilog.

A full adder accepts three inputs:

- `A`
- `B`
- `Cin` — Carry-in

and produces two outputs:

- `Sum`
- `Cout` — Carry-out

The full adder was constructed from the lower-level logic rather than treating it as a black-box component.

The completed Full Adder was then reused as a building block in the next project.

---

# 3. 8-bit Ripple-Carry Adder

The main project in this progression was an **8-bit Ripple-Carry Adder**.

Instead of implementing the entire 8-bit addition operation as one block, I created the design by connecting **eight instances of the Full Adder module** that I had previously developed.

Conceptually:

```text
 A[0] ──┐
 B[0] ──┤
 Cin ───┤
        ▼
     Full Adder ── Sum[0]
        │
       C1
        ▼
     Full Adder ── Sum[1]
        │
       C2
        ▼
       ...
        │
        ▼
     Full Adder ── Sum[7]
        │
       Cout
```

The Verilog architecture followed a modular approach, with instances such as:

```verilog
full_adder fa0 (...);
full_adder fa1 (...);
...
full_adder fa7 (...);
```

Each stage receives the carry generated by the preceding stage. The carry therefore propagates from the least significant bit toward the most significant bit.

### Inputs and Outputs

- `A[7:0]`
- `B[7:0]`
- `Cin`
- `Sum[7:0]`
- `Cout`

This project helped me understand how a larger digital circuit can be constructed hierarchically from smaller reusable modules.

---

# 4. Verification and Simulation

I created a dedicated **Verilog testbench** for the 8-bit adder and compiled and simulated the project using **Icarus Verilog**.

The workflow was:

```text
Verilog source
      ↓
Icarus Verilog
      ↓
Simulation
      ↓
Waveform dump
      ↓
GTKWave
```

I used **GTKWave** to inspect the resulting digital waveforms and observe:

- Input operands
- Carry-in
- Sum bits
- Final carry-out
- Signal changes during simulation

This allowed me to visually verify the behavior of the circuit and see how the signals propagated through the Full Adder stages.

# 5. What I Learned

### Modular Digital Design

The Full Adder was designed independently and then reused as a component of the 8-bit design. This introduced the idea of building larger hardware systems from smaller reusable modules.

### Hierarchical Design

The project followed a progression:

```text
Logic Gates
     ↓
Full Adder
     ↓
8-bit Ripple-Carry Adder
```

### Carry Propagation

Building the Ripple-Carry Adder made the concept of carry propagation tangible. The carry generated by one bit becomes the carry-in for the next bit, allowing eight 1-bit additions to work together as an 8-bit arithmetic circuit.

### RTL and Hardware Simulation

Writing the design in Verilog and observing its behavior in GTKWave gave me practical exposure to describing hardware and verifying its behavior through simulation.
